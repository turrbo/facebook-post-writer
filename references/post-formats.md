## The "See More" Fold

The single most important mechanical detail on Facebook. Posts longer than ~400-480 characters (roughly 3-4 lines on mobile) get truncated with "See More." Your post's success depends on what's above that fold.

### Above the Fold (First 400 characters):
- The hook: most interesting, emotional, or surprising part
- NOT context, background, or "I've been thinking about..."
- Should make someone tap "See More"
- A question, a bold statement, an emotional moment, or a mid-story entry

### What Works Above the Fold:
- "I quit my job on Friday." (immediate, raises questions)
- "Has anyone else's kid suddenly refused to eat anything that isn't beige?" (specific, relatable)
- "I've been on hold with my insurance company for TWO HOURS." (emotional, specific)
- "Update for those who've been following the house situation:" (callback to previous story)

### What Fails Above the Fold:
- "I wanted to share something that happened to me recently..." (vague, nothing to hook)
- "So I know I don't post much, but..." (meta, not interesting)
- "Happy Monday everyone! I hope you're all having a great start to the week." (filler)
- Background context that takes 3 paragraphs to get to the point

## Personal Profile Update

The most common format. Sharing something from your life with people who know you.

### Flow:
1. **The Thing** (1-2 sentences): What happened or what you're feeling. Above the fold
2. **The Context** (2-4 sentences): Background if needed. Keep it tight
3. **The Real Part** (2-5 sentences): The honest, messy, vulnerable, or funny middle
4. **The Landing** (1-2 sentences): Where you are now. Not a lesson -- just current state

### Key Principles:
- Write like you're texting your group chat but slightly more public
- Include the detail your brain said "don't share that" about
- Emoji go at the END of emotional statements, not throughout
- Line breaks between distinct thoughts
- End by trailing off, asking for help, or making a joke -- never inspirational

### Length: 150-800 characters (short wins; long is fine if the story demands it)

## Group Discussion Starter

Getting a conversation going in a group. The goal is comments, not reactions.

### Flow:
1. **The Question or Observation** (1-2 sentences): Specific enough to answer. Above the fold
2. **Your Context** (2-3 sentences): Why you're asking. What you've already tried or thought about
3. **The Specific Ask** (1 sentence): What exactly you want from people

### Key Principles:
- Specific questions get 10x more engagement than vague ones
- Share YOUR situation -- don't just ask in the abstract
- "What's your experience with X?" works better than "What do you think about X?"
- Include what you've already tried (shows effort, prevents basic answers)
- One question per post. Multiple questions = nobody answers any

### Examples:
- "Those of you who work from home with a toddler -- HOW? I start work at 8 and by 8:15 there's a small person on my lap demanding crackers. Daycare waitlist is 6 months. What did you do in the meantime?"
- "Looking for a plumber in [area] who won't charge $500 to look at a dripping faucet. Last guy wanted $400 before even diagnosing the problem. Who do you use?"

## Group Question Post

Asking for specific help or recommendations.

### Flow:
1. **What You Need** (1 sentence): Crystal clear. Above the fold
2. **Context** (2-3 sentences): Location, budget, constraints, what you've tried
3. **Specific Parameters** (1-2 sentences): Any requirements or preferences

### Key Principles:
- Lead with the ask, not the backstory
- Include location for anything local
- Budget range helps people give useful answers
- "I've already tried X" prevents redundant suggestions
- Thank people in comments, not in the original post (saves fold space)

### Example:
"Need a recommendation for a mechanic in north [city] who works on older cars. I have a 2006 Accord that needs brake work and the dealership quoted me $1,200 which feels insane. Budget is ideally under $500. Anyone have a shop they trust?"

## Photo/Video Caption

The image does the talking. The caption adds context, personality, or humor.

### Patterns:

**The One-Liner:**
"10 years of friendship and she still makes me retake every photo"

**The Context + Emotion:**
"First day at the new house. Keys in hand. Still doesn't feel real"

**The Humor:**
"Pinterest vs. reality" (with side-by-side)

**The Minimal:**
Just the year: "2024" or just a heart emoji. Sometimes less is more.

**The Story Caption:**
"This is the view from the spot where I sat and cried after getting the news. Came back a year later. Different tears this time"

### Key Principles:
- Let the photo carry the weight. The caption adds flavor, not a paragraph
- One sentence captions often outperform long ones
- Humor in captions gets more shares
- Inside jokes that your friends get are perfect
- Don't describe what people can see in the photo
- Sentimental is OK on Facebook (more than other platforms) but specific > generic

## Life Milestone Post

New job, graduation, baby, engagement, house, big achievement.

### Flow:
1. **The News** (1-2 sentences): What happened. Keep it direct. Above the fold
2. **The Real Story** (3-5 sentences): The messy behind-the-scenes. How long it really took. What almost went wrong
3. **The Specific Gratitude** (1-2 sentences): Name actual people, not "everyone who supported me"
4. **The Honest Ending** (1 sentence): How you actually feel -- including the scared or uncertain part

### Key Principles:
- The announcement is boring. The STORY is interesting
- Always include what almost went wrong or how long it really took
- Name names in gratitude (specific > generic)
- Admitting you're scared or uncertain alongside excitement is what makes it real
- "I still can't believe it" is honest. "Beyond blessed" is not
- Skip: "Excited to announce" / "Thrilled to share" / "New chapter"

## Vent/Frustration Post

Getting something off your chest. Facebook audiences are generally supportive.

### Flow:
1. **The Frustration** (1-2 sentences): What happened. Specific. Emotional. Above the fold
2. **The Escalation** (2-4 sentences): The details that make it worse. The absurdity
3. **The Ask or Release** (1-2 sentences): Either ask for help or just acknowledge you needed to say it

### Key Principles:
- Specific frustrations are relatable. Vague frustrations are vague-posting
- Humor mixed with frustration is Facebook gold
- Name the company/situation, not the individual
- "Am I being unreasonable?" invites supportive comments
- End with either a practical ask ("does anyone have a contact at...") or a self-aware sign-off ("ok rant over")
- Don't vague-post ("some people just don't get it"). It annoys everyone

## Page Post (Business)

For business pages. Needs to balance professionalism with Facebook's warm culture.

### Key Principles:
- Write like a person, not a brand
- First person > third person ("We just shipped..." not "[Company] is pleased to announce...")
- Behind-the-scenes content outperforms polished marketing
- Show the humans (team photos, candid moments, real workspace)
- Questions get more engagement than announcements
- Don't link out in every post -- Facebook suppresses external links

### Patterns That Work:
- Behind-the-scenes photos with casual caption
- "We messed up and here's what we're doing about it" (transparency)
- Customer stories told with permission
- Simple question related to your field
- "This or that" format with product/service images

## Comment Replies

How you respond to comments on your posts.

### Principles:
- Reply to early comments quickly (signals engagement to algorithm)
- Be specific in replies, not just "Thanks!" or "So true!"
- Ask follow-up questions to keep conversation going
- Reference what the commenter specifically said
- Humor and warmth go further than formality

### Good Replies:
- "Wait seriously?? I had no idea. Going to try this tomorrow"
- "This is exactly what we ended up doing and it worked!"
- "Ugh yes. When did it get better for you?"
- "Haha ok that makes me feel so much better"
- "Oh my gosh [name] thank you, I'm going to call them today"

### Bad Replies:
- "Thank you for sharing your experience!"
- "Great point! I really appreciate your input."
- "I completely agree with everything you've said."
- "Thanks for the insightful comment!"

## Anti-Patterns (Posts That Feel AI-Generated)

### The LinkedIn Import
Corporate language, professional milestones announced formally, "Excited to share" openings. This screams "I wrote this on LinkedIn and copied it to Facebook."
**Fix:** Rewrite as if you're telling your friends at dinner.

### The Motivational Poster
Inspirational quote energy. "Remember: every day is a new beginning." Real people don't talk like this unless they're going through something very specific.
**Fix:** Delete the inspiration. Share the specific moment that made you feel that way.

### The Engagement Bot
"What's your favorite [noun]? Drop it in the comments!" with nothing personal attached. Algorithm bait that Facebook now actively suppresses.
**Fix:** Share YOUR answer first and make it personal. Then ask.

### The Perfect Update
Everything went well. Everyone is grateful. Life is beautiful. No rough edges. No uncertainty.
**Fix:** Include one honest complication. One "but also..." One admission of doubt.

### The Over-Formatted Post
Emoji bullet points, numbered lists, bold text, line breaks every sentence. Looks like a content creator template, not a person posting.
**Fix:** Write in natural paragraphs with organic line breaks.

### The Vague-Post
"Some people just... wow." Designed to make people ask "what happened?" in comments. Universally annoying.
**Fix:** If you're going to share, share the actual thing. If you can't, don't post.

## Local Community / Buy-Sell-Trade Groups

**Tone:** Neighborly, direct, sometimes dramatic
**Post Length:** Short to medium. Get to the point
**What works:** Clear asks, helpful info, genuine recommendations, neighborhood alerts
**What doesn't:** Long personal essays, political hot takes, corporate marketing

### Culture Notes
- These groups are hyperlocal. Reference specific streets, neighborhoods, businesses
- "Anyone know a good [service provider] near [area]?" is the bread and butter
- Drama happens (noise complaints, parking disputes) -- stay out of it in group posts
- Photos matter for selling items -- good lighting, multiple angles
- Price transparency is expected. "DM for price" frustrates people
- Admins are active and will remove promotional content fast

### Language Patterns
- "ISO" (in search of)
- "Does anyone know..."
- "PSA for anyone in [area]"
- Tagging neighborhood names
- "Still available" / "pending" / "sold"

## Parenting Groups

**Tone:** Supportive, vulnerable, occasionally desperate at 3am
**Post Length:** Medium to long. Context matters
**What works:** Honest struggles, specific questions, solidarity, humor about parenting chaos
**What doesn't:** Judgment, unsolicited advice framed as universal truth, humble bragging about kids

### Culture Notes
- Vulnerability is currency. "I yelled at my kid today and I feel terrible" gets massive support
- "Is this normal?" is the most common post format -- and always gets engagement
- Unsolicited advice is the #1 trigger for conflict. Frame everything as "what worked for me"
- Sleep, feeding, and school posts get the most comments
- Age-specific groups (newborn, toddler, teen) have very different energy
- Humor about parenting chaos is universally loved
- Nobody wants to hear your kid is "gifted" unless they specifically asked

### Language Patterns
- "Is this normal?"
- "Please tell me I'm not the only one"
- "Am I a terrible parent for..."
- "What age did your kid..."
- "SOS" / "help" / "I'm losing it"
- "No judgment please"

## Career & Professional Groups

**Tone:** Supportive but practical, more personal than LinkedIn
**Post Length:** Medium. Enough context for good advice
**What works:** Specific situations with details, salary transparency, interview horror stories, honest career questions
**What doesn't:** LinkedIn-style self-promotion, vague advice, "just network" platitudes

### Culture Notes
- Facebook career groups are warmer than LinkedIn -- people share real struggles
- Salary sharing is encouraged and creates massive engagement
- "Should I take this job?" with specific pros/cons gets thoughtful responses
- Resume reviews get engagement if you show vulnerability
- Interview disaster stories are bonding moments
- Anti-corporate sentiment is common but not as aggressive as Reddit

### Language Patterns
- "Would you take this job?"
- "Am I being lowballed?"
- "How do I handle [specific work situation]?"
- "Is this a red flag?"
- "I need to vent about work for a sec"

## Hobby & Interest Groups

**Tone:** Enthusiastic, welcoming to beginners, passionate about the craft
**Post Length:** Short to medium. Photos/videos often carry the post
**What works:** Progress photos, beginner questions, failures shared with humor, recommendations
**What doesn't:** Gatekeeping, over-technical jargon without context, obvious promotion

### Culture Notes
- "I'm new here" posts get showered with support
- Progress photos (before/after, first attempt vs. latest) perform extremely well
- Asking for critique with genuine openness gets engagement
- Sharing failures ("my first attempt at bread... nailed it" + disaster photo) is beloved
- Equipment/tool debates are passionate but usually friendly
- Experienced members who help beginners earn community status

### Subtypes and Nuances
- **Cooking/baking:** Recipe sharing, "what went wrong" troubleshooting, pantry raids
- **Gardening:** Zone-specific advice, pest identification, harvest brags
- **Fitness:** Progress pics, form checks, program recommendations
- **Crafts:** WIP (work in progress) photos, material sourcing, pattern sharing
- **Gaming:** Screenshots, build showcases, "looking for group" posts

## Health & Wellness Groups

**Tone:** Deeply supportive, vulnerable, careful with medical claims
**Post Length:** Medium to long. People share detailed experiences
**What works:** Personal health journeys, "has anyone experienced this," emotional support, practical tips
**What doesn't:** Medical advice stated as fact, miracle cure claims, shaming

### Culture Notes
- These groups are often deeply personal. People share things here they don't share elsewhere
- "Has anyone else experienced [symptom/situation]?" gets immediate responses
- Always frame health information as personal experience, not medical advice
- Weight loss / fitness groups have specific sensitivities around language
- Mental health groups value "me too" solidarity over advice-giving
- Chronic illness groups have established patterns: good days, bad days, medication updates
- Admins heavily moderate health claims and promotional content

### Language Patterns
- "Has anyone else..."
- "Diagnosed with [condition], feeling scared"
- "Update on my [condition]"
- "What helped you with..."
- "Need encouragement today"
- "Small win:" (celebrating minor progress)

## Entrepreneurship & Side Hustle Groups

**Tone:** Hustle-positive but grounded, supportive of small wins
**Post Length:** Medium. Numbers help
**What works:** Revenue milestones (any size), specific problems with context, tool recommendations, honest failures
**What doesn't:** Vague success claims, "DM me," MLM energy, course selling

### Culture Notes
- Small wins get celebrated hard ($100 first sale = standing ovation)
- "How do I..." questions with specific context get excellent advice
- Revenue transparency builds trust (share real numbers)
- Anti-MLM sentiment is strong. Anything that smells like MLM gets flagged
- "What's working for you right now?" is a reliable engagement format
- Admins in good groups heavily police self-promotion
- "I failed" posts with lessons get more respect than humble brags

### Language Patterns
- "Just hit $X MRR!"
- "What's working for you right now?"
- "How do you handle [specific business problem]?"
- "Be honest: is this idea dumb?"
- "Month X update"
- "First sale!!"

## Tech & Developer Groups

**Tone:** Helpful, occasionally snarky, knowledge-sharing focused
**Post Length:** Short to medium. Code snippets and screenshots help
**What works:** Specific error messages, debugging help, tool comparisons, career transitions
**What doesn't:** "Google it" responses, gatekeeping, vague questions without context

### Culture Notes
- "What's wrong with my code" posts need sufficient context (language, error, what you tried)
- Tool debates (VS Code vs. JetBrains, React vs. Vue) are passionate
- Career transition posts (bootcamp grad, self-taught) get supportive responses
- "What should I learn next?" needs context about current skills and goals
- Memes and humor are welcome in most groups
- Answering your own question after finding the solution is respected

## Home & DIY Groups

**Tone:** Practical, photo-heavy, supportive of all skill levels
**Post Length:** Short with photos. Context about the house/project matters
**What works:** Before/after photos, "is this normal" questions, contractor horror stories, budget breakdowns
**What doesn't:** Bragging without process, unrealistic expectations, dismissing safety concerns

### Culture Notes
- Before/after photos are the #1 engagement driver
- "Is this [thing in my house] a problem?" with a photo always gets responses
- Budget transparency (how much things cost) is extremely valued
- Contractor stories (good and bad) get engagement
- DIY disasters shared with humor are beloved
- "First time doing [task]" gets encouraging responses
- Always include location for contractor recommendations (trades are local)

### Language Patterns
- "Is this normal?" + photo
- "Before and after"
- "How much should I expect to pay for..."
- "DIY or hire a pro?"
- "What would you do with this space?"
- "Found this when we opened the wall... help"

## Personal Finance Groups

**Tone:** Supportive, judgment-free (ideally), practical
**Post Length:** Medium. Specific numbers help
**What works:** Debt payoff milestones, budget questions, "am I being scammed," investment questions for beginners
**What doesn't:** Shaming people for financial situations, crypto shilling, get-rich-quick energy

### Culture Notes
- Debt payoff milestones get massive celebration regardless of amount
- "Is this a good deal?" posts with specific numbers get practical responses
- Budget breakdowns with real numbers build trust and engagement
- People share financial struggles here they wouldn't share on their personal profile
- Anti-predatory-lending and anti-scam content performs well
- "Dumb question but..." gets reassurance that it's not dumb

## General Group Posting Strategy

### Before Posting in Any Group
1. Read the group rules (pinned post or About section)
2. Search if your question has been asked recently
3. Lurk for a few days to understand the tone
4. Note which posts get engagement and which get ignored
5. Check if self-promotion is allowed and how

### Universal Group Post Principles
- **Be specific.** "I need help with my 2019 Honda Civic making a clicking sound when I turn left" beats "Car is making a weird noise"
- **Show you tried.** "I googled this and found X but I'm not sure if it applies because Y" builds goodwill
- **Include location when relevant.** Many recommendations are location-dependent
- **Photos help.** Almost every type of group post benefits from a photo
- **Reply to your own comments.** Stay engaged in the conversation you started
- **Thank people.** A simple "this worked, thank you!" follow-up builds community

### Posting Times
| Window | Why | Best For |
|--------|-----|----------|
| 7-9 AM | Morning scroll with coffee | Short updates, questions |
| 12-1 PM | Lunch break | Discussion posts |
| 7-9 PM | Evening wind-down | Longer posts, stories, photos |
| Weekends | Casual browsing | Life updates, hobby posts, DIY projects |

### Engagement Patterns
- Posts with photos get 2-3x more engagement than text-only
- Questions get more comments than statements
- "Is this normal?" format works in almost every group
- People engage more with vulnerability than with expertise
- Follow-up "update" posts get strong engagement from people who remember the original

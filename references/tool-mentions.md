# Tool/Product Mention Guidelines (Facebook Edition)

Only mention tools if user requests it. Default is NO tool mentions.

---

## Key Difference from Other Platforms

Facebook is the most relational platform. People know each other. A tool mention on Facebook feels like a friend recommending something -- or like a friend who secretly started selling essential oils. The line is thin and the social cost of crossing it is high. On Reddit or X.com, a bad promo gets a downvote or a mute. On Facebook, it changes how your friends see you.

In groups, self-promotion is the #1 reason people get removed.

---

## Subtlety Levels

### Level 1-2 (Barely There)
"My sister set up some app thing on our phones and now the kids' schedules just show up on my calendar somehow. I don't know how it works but I haven't missed a pickup in 2 weeks so I'm not questioning it"
- Tool not named
- Attributed to someone else
- Actively doesn't understand it
- Small specific benefit

### Level 3-4 (Brief Mention)
"A friend recommended [tool] for tracking expenses. I've been using it for maybe a month? It's fine. Not life-changing but I stopped losing receipts which was the main problem"
- Named once, casually
- Hedged immediately ("it's fine")
- Specific small benefit (stopped losing receipts)
- Honest timeline (maybe a month)

### Level 5-6 (Noticeable but Not Dominant)
"So I've been trying to get more organized (lol I know, me) and I started using [tool] for [specific task]. It's got some annoying quirks -- like it crashes if you try to [specific thing] -- but for the basic stuff it's been helping. Jury's still out on whether I'll keep it past the free trial honestly"
- More detail but honest
- Specific limitation (crashes)
- Not committed to it (jury's still out, free trial)
- Self-deprecating context

### Level 7-10 (Don't Use -- Too Promotional)
Will get you removed from groups, unfollowed by friends, and suspected of being a bot or MLM rep. The social cost on Facebook is higher than any other platform.

## Placement Rules

### Where to Put It:
- **Deep in the post** (well after the fold)
- **Inside a personal story** (the tool is a detail, not the point)
- **As one thing among many** ("I tried X, asked my neighbor, called the company, AND started using [tool]")
- **In response to someone else's question** (not unprompted)

### Where NOT to Put It:
- Above the "See More" fold (immediately looks like an ad)
- As the turning point of a story
- In the first comment (looks like planned promo)
- In the post AND a comment (repetition = ad)
- Unprompted in a group (will get flagged)

---

## Language Patterns

### Phrases That Work:
- "My [person] set me up with [tool]"
- "Someone in this group actually mentioned [tool] a while back"
- "I've been messing around with [tool], it's ok"
- "Not sure if [tool] is actually good or if I just needed any system at all"

### Phrases That DON'T Work:
- "I discovered [tool] and it changed everything"
- "If you're looking for [solution], try [tool]!"
- "Highly recommend [tool]"
- "Game-changer!"
- "Check out [tool]" (even casually)
- "Full disclosure, I use [tool]" (paradoxically more suspicious)
- "Not sponsored lol" (nobody believes this)

---

## Required Disclaimers

Include at least one:
- "Honestly not sure if it's that great"
- "It crashes sometimes"
- "Jury's still out"
- "My [person] set it up, I couldn't do it myself"
- "Might just be me"
- "Could probably do this with a spreadsheet"

---

## Promotion Test

**Critical:** Remove the tool mention entirely. Does the post still:
1. Tell a complete story?
2. Sound like something you'd actually post?
3. Help the reader or contribute to the conversation?

If NO to any, rewrite with the story first.

---

## Facebook-Specific Risks

- **Group removal:** Admins in good groups actively remove promotional posts
- **Social cost:** Friends notice when you start "selling" on your personal profile
- **MLM suspicion:** Any product recommendation on Facebook triggers MLM radar, especially from women (unfair but real)
- **Algorithm penalty:** Facebook actively suppresses posts with promotional intent
- **Trust erosion:** One obviously sponsored post makes friends question your future recommendations
- **Comment pile-on:** Group members will publicly call out perceived promotions

### The MLM Test
Would a reasonable person reading this think you're selling something? If there's any chance, dial it back further. Facebook users have been burned by too many "hey girl" DMs to give anyone the benefit of the doubt on product mentions.

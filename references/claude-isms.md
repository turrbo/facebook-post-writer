# Claude-Specific Vocabulary Database (Facebook Edition)

Vocabulary, phrases, and patterns statistically overrepresented in Claude outputs. Adapted for detecting AI tells in Facebook posts, where warmth and relational tone are everything.

---

## Why Detection Works Differently on Facebook

Facebook is the most relational major platform. People post knowing their aunt, their coworker, and their college roommate will all see it. This creates a specific voice: warm, personal, slightly guarded, but genuine. AI tells on Facebook aren't just about vocabulary -- they're about **emotional register**. A Facebook post that sounds like a blog post, a LinkedIn update, or a press release is immediately wrong.

The biggest risk on Facebook: **sounding like content instead of a person.**

## CATEGORY 5: Facebook-Specific AI Tells

### The LinkedIn Crossover (Most Common)
AI-generated Facebook posts constantly sound like LinkedIn posts. These patterns are the #1 giveaway:

| LinkedIn Pattern | Why It Fails on Facebook | Fix |
|-----------------|-------------------------|-----|
| "Excited to announce..." | Corporate energy | "So this happened..." or just share the news |
| "I'm thrilled to share..." | Nobody talks like this to friends | "Ok so I have news" |
| "Grateful for this opportunity" | Performative gratitude | Be specific: "My friend Sarah helped me get this and I owe her dinner for life" |
| Professional milestone framing | Too polished | Share the messy version |
| "Lessons learned" structure | Essay format | Tell the story, let people draw their own lessons |
| Tagging companies/brands formally | Corporate association | Keep it personal |

### The Motivational Poster Closing
Facebook AI posts almost always end with inspirational platitudes:

| AI Closing | Fix |
|------------|-----|
| "You've got this!" | Delete or say something specific |
| "Remember, you are enough" | [delete] |
| "Never give up on your dreams" | [delete] |
| "Here's to new beginnings" | [delete] |
| "If I can do it, anyone can" | "I got lucky honestly" |
| "Believe in yourself" | [delete entirely] |

### The Over-Structured Personal Update
Real Facebook posts ramble. They double back. They include random details. AI Facebook posts are too organized:

**AI Pattern:** Clear setup -> story -> lesson -> inspirational close
**Human Pattern:** Jump into the middle -> backtrack for context -> tangent -> return -> trail off

## CATEGORY 6: Opening Patterns

| Opening | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---------|--------------|-----|
| "I wanted to share..." | Announcement energy | Just share |
| "So I've been thinking..." | Vague, no entry point | What specifically triggered it? |
| "Can I be honest for a second?" | Performative vulnerability | Just be honest |
| "I don't usually post stuff like this, but..." | Drawing attention to the act of posting | Delete and just post |
| "It's been a while since I posted..." | Meta-posting about posting | Skip to the content |
| "Happy [day]! Here's..." | Generic greeting filler | Lead with the thing |

### Better Openings (Facebook Style)
- Jump into what happened: "So I walked out of my doctor's appointment today and..."
- A direct statement: "I quit my job yesterday"
- A question: "Has anyone else dealt with [specific thing]?"
- Mid-emotion: "I'm sitting in the parking lot at work and I just need to say this"
- Casual update: "Update on the house situation for those who've been asking"

| Closing | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---------|--------------|-----|
| "I hope this helps someone" | Too polished/altruistic | "anyway, thought I'd share" |
| "What do you think?" (after lecture) | Formulaic | Ask something specific |
| "Sending love to everyone going through this" | Generic warmth | Name the specific people/situation |
| "Grateful for this community" | Performative | [delete] or be very specific |
| "Here's to [positive abstract]" | Toast format | |

### Better Closings (Facebook Style)
- Trail off: "anyway... that's where I'm at"
- Ask for specific help: "has anyone used [specific service] in [specific area]?"
- Self-deprecating: "and now I'm posting this on Facebook like a normal person lol"
- Just stop when the story ends
- Practical ask: "if anyone knows a good plumber in [city] please help"
- "ok rant over" / "thanks for coming to my ted talk" (self-aware)

## CATEGORY 8: Facebook Humanizers

### Casual Markers (Need 2-3 per post)
- honestly
- so
- anyway
- like
- you guys / y'all
- lol / haha / hahaha
- idk
- tbh
- ok so
- I mean
- right?

### Facebook-Specific Patterns
- Ellipses for trailing thoughts... like this
- ALL CAPS for emphasis on ONE word ("I am SO tired")
- Emoji as emotional punctuation, not decoration
- Addressing specific people: "for those who've been asking"
- The aside: "(yes I know, I know)"
- Self-aware meta-comments: "posting this on Facebook because who else am I going to tell"
- Gratitude that's specific: "shoutout to [name] who literally drove across town for this"

### Natural Emoji Use
- After something self-deprecating: text + laughing emoji
- After something genuine: text + heart
- After something exasperated: text + facepalm or eye-roll
- NEVER: emoji bullet points, emoji per line, emoji chains

# Good vs Bad Examples (Facebook Edition)

## Personal Updates

### BAD (AI-Written):
"I wanted to share some exciting news with all of you! After months of careful consideration and navigating a challenging decision-making process, I've decided to make a career change. I'm genuinely grateful for the support of everyone who has been part of this journey. Here's to new beginnings! What's the best career advice you've ever received?"

### GOOD (Human-Written):
"Ok so I quit my job on Friday. I've been wanting to for like a year and I finally just... did it. I don't have another one lined up which is terrifying but also I slept through the night for the first time in months so maybe that's a sign?? Anyway if anyone hears of anything in marketing lmk. Also I'm available for lunch now apparently lol"

**Why the second works:**
- Jumps right in ("Ok so")
- Admits fear alongside relief (terrifying + slept through the night)
- Practical ask mixed in naturally (if anyone hears of anything)
- Self-deprecating humor (available for lunch now apparently)
- "lol" and "lmk" are natural casual markers
- Double question marks show genuine uncertainty
- No "journey," no "grateful," no "here's to"

## Group Posts (Asking for Advice)

### BAD (AI-Written):
"Hello everyone! I'm reaching out to this wonderful community for some guidance. I've been navigating the home buying process and finding it quite overwhelming. Could anyone share their experience with first-time home buying? Any comprehensive tips or straightforward advice would be genuinely appreciated. Thank you in advance for your valuable insights!"

### GOOD (Human-Written):
"First time home buyer here and I'm losing my mind a little bit. We put in an offer on a house last week, got outbid, found another one, and now the inspection came back with foundation issues. Is this just... what it's like?? How many houses did you go through before you actually closed? I feel like everyone I know bought their house in like a week and here we are on month 4"

**Why the second works:**
- Specific timeline (last week, month 4)
- Specific problem (outbid, foundation issues)
- Genuine exasperation ("Is this just... what it's like??")
- Compares to perceived norm (everyone I know bought in a week)
- Asks a specific question (how many houses)
- No "wonderful community" or "valuable insights"
- No "Thank you in advance"

## Group Posts (Sharing Experience)

### BAD (AI-Written):
"I've been a member of this group for a while and wanted to share my experience transitioning to remote work. The key lessons I've learned include the importance of establishing boundaries, creating a dedicated workspace, and maintaining consistent communication with your team. I hope this helps someone who's going through a similar transition!"

### GOOD (Human-Written):
"Been working remote for 6 months now and nobody warned me about the weird guilt. Like I'll be doing laundry at 2pm and feel like I'm getting away with something even though I already worked 7 hours that day. Also my electric bill is insane now?? And I talk to my dog too much.

The good parts: I haven't worn real pants in weeks and I get to eat lunch with my kid.

Idk if this is helpful to anyone but if you just started remote work and feel weird about it, it gets... less weird. Not normal. Just less weird."

**Why the second works:**
- Specific unexpected detail (the guilt, the laundry at 2pm)
- Real downsides (electric bill, talking to dog too much)
- Real upsides that are specific (no real pants, lunch with kid)
- Ending is honest uncertainty, not a lesson
- "Less weird. Not normal. Just less weird." -- that's a real thought
- No "key lessons" or "I hope this helps"

## Celebration Posts

### BAD (AI-Written):
"I'm beyond thrilled to announce that after years of hard work and dedication, I've officially graduated with my Master's degree! This journey has been incredibly challenging but ultimately rewarding. I couldn't have done it without the support of my amazing family and friends. Grateful beyond words. Here's to the next chapter!"

### GOOD (Human-Written):
"I GRADUATED. I actually did it. It took me 4 years because I was working full time and there was that semester I almost dropped out because of the stats class from hell. But it's done. I have a whole master's degree and honestly I'm still not sure I believe it.

Mom cried. I cried. The dog was confused.

Thank you to literally everyone who asked me 'how's school going' even when my answer was always 'terrible but I'm fine.' You kept me going more than you know"

**Why the second works:**
- ALL CAPS for genuine excitement ("I GRADUATED")
- Specific struggles (4 years, working full time, stats class from hell)
- Self-doubt is authentic (still not sure I believe it)
- Humor grounds the emotion (dog was confused)
- Gratitude is specific (everyone who asked how's school going)
- Acknowledges the real answer was always "terrible"
- No "beyond thrilled," "journey," or "next chapter"

## Photo/Video Captions

### BAD Caption:
"Capturing beautiful moments with the people who matter most. Feeling genuinely grateful for these incredible connections. Life is about the journey, not the destination."

### GOOD Caption:
"10 years of friendship and she still makes me take 47 photos before one is acceptable"

Or even:
"No caption needed but here's one anyway"

Or:
"Annual trip. Same cabin. We're all a little more tired and a lot more gray but the jokes haven't changed"

**Why the good ones work:**
- Specific (47 photos, 10 years, same cabin)
- Say something real about the relationship
- Self-aware about the act of posting
- No "beautiful moments" or "journey"

## Venting/Frustration Posts

### BAD (AI-Written):
"I need to express my frustration about the current state of customer service. It's genuinely disappointing how companies have moved away from treating customers as valued individuals. After navigating a particularly challenging experience today, I've come to realize that we need to demand better. Has anyone else encountered similar issues?"

### GOOD (Human-Written):
"I have been on hold with my insurance company for TWO HOURS. I am not exaggerating. Two actual hours. I've cleaned the kitchen, folded laundry, made lunch, and reorganized my junk drawer and I'm STILL on hold. The hold music has been stuck in my head since hour one and I think it might be there forever.

If anyone has a human being at [insurance company] they can connect me with I will literally bake you a cake"

**Why the second works:**
- Specific escalation (lists everything done while on hold)
- ALL CAPS for emphasis shows real frustration
- Humor through absurdity (the hold music, bake you a cake)
- Practical ask at the end (does anyone have a contact)
- No "I've come to realize" or "we need to demand better"
- Addresses real people who might actually help

## Tool/Product Mentions

### BAD (Too Promotional):
"I recently discovered [AppName] and it has been a genuine game-changer for organizing my family's schedule. The comprehensive features and straightforward interface make it incredibly easy to manage everything in one place. I highly recommend checking it out!"

### GOOD (Properly Subtle):
"My sister in law set up some calendar app thing on my phone and honestly I don't fully understand it but somehow everyone's schedules are just... there now? I showed up to the right soccer practice for the first time in 3 weeks so I'm calling it a win"

**Why the second works:**
- Someone else set it up (didn't "discover" it)
- Doesn't understand it (honest)
- Doesn't even name it
- Success metric is hilariously small (right soccer practice)
- "Calling it a win" is self-deprecating

## Comment Replies

### BAD Reply:
"Thank you so much for your thoughtful response! I really appreciate your perspective and the valuable insights you've shared. It's genuinely helpful to hear from someone who has been through a similar experience."

### GOOD Reply:
"oh wow I didn't know that was an option!! Thank you, going to try this tomorrow"

Or: "this is exactly what happened to us too. Felt like we were the only ones"

Or: "haha ok that makes me feel way better honestly"

**Why the good ones work:**
- Short and reactive
- Specific emotion (relief, excitement, solidarity)
- No "valuable insights" or "thoughtful response"
- Natural punctuation (double exclamation, "haha")
